## Target and matched training conditions

For artifact i, the judge sees the original Python source, candidate Lean artifact and gold-reference artifact that correspond to the human task. Record exactly which proof bodies are visible and hash-bind the delivered inputs. Predict a six-category probability mass function q_i(r), r in {0,1,2,3,4,5}, for a rating from the declared qualified human population or fixed panel. A sampled histogram of three scalar judge calls is not a native probability forecast.

| Condition | Training objective | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Method 2 — Pearson | Maximize training Pearson correlation between predicted expected ratings and human mean ratings. | Initial correlation comparator; fix zero-variance handling, convergence checks and any tie rule before fitting. |
| Method 4 — RPS | Minimize mean individual-rating normalized ranked probability score (RPS). | Primary proper-loss condition for the ordinal target. |
| Method 4 — CE | Minimize mean individual-rating cross-entropy (CE). | Separate condition with the same probability representation and declared probability-floor policy. |
| Method 4 — Brier | Minimize mean individual-rating six-category Brier loss. | Separate condition; use the sum over six categories, not binary prover Brier loss. |

Spearman or Kendall training may be added as separately implemented and verified conditions; reporting their test correlations does not mean they were optimized during training. Pearson describes linear association **between the output ratings**. A neural judge or its mapping from artifacts to ratings may be nonlinear; maximizing Pearson does not constrain that mapping to be linear. High correlation can coexist with substantial signed bias, absolute error, or an incorrect probability distribution.

Use the same train inputs, human observations, probability representation, model family/capacity, backbone and harness, initialization/seed policy where applicable, allowed tools, candidate/search space and resource ceilings. Identify whether an intervention fits a prediction head, optimizes prompts or trains model parameters; do not present these as interchangeable interventions. Match opportunities for any train-fitted probability mapping and regularization. Record actual resource usage, convergence failures, missing forecasts and all declared attempts. Differences needed by numerical optimizers must be explicit; equal step counts alone do not establish equal optimization quality.

For every condition, select hyperparameters, stopping and prompt candidates by the **same artifact-weighted validation RPS**. Fix the search budget, tie policy and practical failure/cost criteria before selection. Freeze selected judges and analysis code before opening test ratings. Test outcomes evaluate the chosen procedures; they do not choose the winning configuration or trigger retries until a preferred objective wins. A future training-by-validation-selector factorial is a separate question, not required to begin this primary comparison.

## Shared held-out evaluation metrics

Training objectives and evaluation metrics are separate. Every fixed judge receives the same panel on the same eligible artifacts and human ratings. Average individual-rating losses within each artifact first, then weight artifacts equally. Retain the full scheduled denominator and report failures/missingness under the prespecified policy. Additional raters on an artifact must not silently increase its weight.

**Evaluation clarification, 11-09-2026:** RPS remains the primary held-out metric and common validation criterion. Cross-entropy (CE) and total variation distance (TVD) are secondary evaluation metrics for every training objective. This prospective panel does not change any completed experiment's primary contrast, protocol or results.

Write p-hat_i for the empirical human rating distribution, F_i(k) for its cumulative distribution function (CDF), Q_i(k) for the judge CDF, and mu_i = sum_r r q_i(r)/5 for the normalized expected judge rating. Let hbar_i be the mean human rating divided by 5. The following are definitions for the new condition, not replacements for historical scorers.

| Metric | Definition and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Individual-rating normalized RPS — primary | For human rating r, (1/5) sum over k=0,...,4 of (Q_i(k) - 1{r <= k})^2; average within artifact and then over artifacts. This remains experiment 02's primary loss and common validation criterion. |
| CE — secondary | Average -log q_i(r) over the same ratings and artifacts; natural-log units. Fix smoothing/floors before evaluation, retain parsing failures, and disclose how zero probability is handled. |
| TVD — secondary | mean_i (1/2) sum_r abs(q_i(r) - p-hat_i(r)); range 0–1, lower is better. Compute the distance to each artifact's empirical human rating distribution, then average artifacts equally. |
| Six-category Brier | Average sum over s=0,...,5 of (q_i(s) - 1{r=s})^2; range 0–2. This differs from the separate binary prover-success score (p-y)^2, range 0–1. |
| Normalized root mean squared error (nRMSE) | sqrt(mean_i (mu_i - hbar_i)^2), using scores divided by 5; range 0–1. |
| Root mean squared CDF distance | sqrt(mean_i (1/5) sum_k (Q_i(k) - F_i(k))^2). Experiment 07 calls this `sqrt RPS`; it compares with the empirical human distribution. |
| Forward Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence | mean_i sum_r p-hat_i(r) log(p-hat_i(r)/q_i(r)), with zero human-mass terms contributing zero. CE minus empirical human entropy, under identical weighting and probability policy. |
| Pearson, Spearman and Kendall | Three distinct correlations of mu_i and hbar_i across artifacts; declare average-rank/tau-b conventions, ties and undefined cases. |
| Mean absolute error (MAE), signed bias and both means | mean_i abs(mu_i-hbar_i); mean_i(mu_i-hbar_i); mean_i mu_i and mean_i hbar_i. Positive bias means higher judge ratings. |
| Constant baselines | Evaluate a train-fitted human-marginal probability forecast on the same panel, including its nRMSE. Keep the experiment 07 in-sample best-constant nRMSE labeled as an optimistic descriptive reference, not a deployable train-fitted baseline. |

**RPS distinction:** for each artifact, the mean individual-rating RPS equals its squared empirical-CDF distance plus (1/5) sum_k F_i(k)(1-F_i(k)). Consequently, experiment 07's `sqrt RPS` takes the square root **after averaging the empirical-CDF distances across artifacts**; it is not the square root of raw individual-rating RPS. The empirical disagreement subtraction is not a claim that a sparse histogram equals the population distribution. Keep both definitions visible and do not silently change the primary endpoint.

**TVD distinction:** this is a distance to a noisy empirical human rating distribution, not a known population distribution and not TVD mutual information (TVD-MI). Do not average distances to individual point-mass ratings: that would be a different metric. TVD does not use the order or spacing of rating categories. Its role here is a secondary distribution diagnostic; RPS remains the ordinal primary loss. Report paired differences with the same artifact weighting and resamples, preserve missing forecasts in the full denominator, and use the range 0–1 for missingness bounds. Such bounds are not confidence intervals.

The [experiment 07 report](../archive/07_compare_5_judges_on_75_outputs_and_estimate_rating_needs_start_09Nov2026_end_09Nov2026/results.md) and [metric implementation](../archive/07_compare_5_judges_on_75_outputs_and_estimate_rating_needs_start_09Nov2026_end_09Nov2026/scripts/exp1_metrics.py) remain historical evidence. Its scalar metrics use the original repeated-score means while its distribution metrics use floored/renormalized sampled histograms. The new native-probability condition derives scalar means from its declared q; these are distinct representations and must not be silently relabeled. Preserve every old floor, split, bootstrap unit and reported result.

Report paired 95% uncertainty for objective comparisons using the same resamples and a procedure that respects task families, reused raters and fixed versus sampled agents. Distinguish full-procedure refitting/selection uncertainty from intervals conditional on fixed predictions. Also report repeat counts and variability of probability forecasts separately from human disagreement. Set the uncertainty procedure and any success margins before test inspection; no numerical deployment threshold is introduced by this note.
