\subsection*{Bottom line}
Yes for the evaluation, not for the judge. Pooling old (25 tasks, 75 artifacts, 195 ratings) and new (25 tasks, 100 artifacts,
247 ratings) data narrows the RPS interval relative to old alone to 0.60$\times$ for GPT-6 Astra and 0.64$\times$ for Claude Opus 5.5.
But the pooled ``judge beats the constant'' result is an artifact of mixing two rating levels: against a constant computed
inside each set, Astra is tied ($+0.002$) and Opus is not clearly better ($-0.018$, $[-0.048, +0.013]$). Both judges overscore
the old set by about half a point, and more data does not change that. Only 247 of 3,300 sheet rows were back, 73\% of them 5s.
Cost: \$0 extra (plans only).

\subsection*{Caveats}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Only 247 of 3,300 sheet rows back at the main run, from 5 raters; 55 artifacts have exactly 2 ratings, so per-artifact human distributions are noisy; 73\% of new ratings are 5s (a ceiling effect).
  \item Old and new tasks differ, harnesses differ between old and new judge calls, only two judges have scores on the new set. No Lean proof check and no differential testing.
  \item Pooled correlations are inflated by the difference in rating level between the sets.
\end{itemize}
